Six disciplines.
One creative language.

The parts of a production belong together. Each workspace is designed around the same living document.

Aether CoreOne source of creative truth.

Your .cdom document connects the whole project. Validated commands, explicit dependencies, revision history, and undo keep meaningful changes inspectable.

Aether LoreCharacters with a past. Stories with a future.

Keep world rules, character states, dialogue, and story branches together. Continuity checks point to the events behind a contradiction.

Aether StageCompose the world, layer by layer.

Build a scene with editable layers, supported SVG geometry, a 2.5D camera, and authored animation. Change one element without replacing the whole scene.

Aether AudioLet the story shape the sound.

Plan stems, dialogue, and spatial Foley on a common timeline. Explicit bindings can connect story tension to the mix and scene coordinates to sound position.

Aether DirectorA collaborator. You keep the final cut.

Review scoped AI suggestions with their context and intended effects. Approve the operations you want, while the canonical document stays under your control.

Aether PublishOne world. More ways to experience it.

Plan horizontal and vertical compositions, captions, interactive episodes, and portable exports from the same project, with creator review before publication.

The planned creative system. Capabilities are in development.

A few things
you might be wondering.

What is AETHER-DOM, exactly?

A creative IDE in development for motion comics, animatics, and interactive stories. The idea is to connect your narrative, scene layers, timeline, and sound through one versioned creative document.

Is this available to use yet?

This is an early design exploration. The studio is not released, and the interaction on this page is an illustrative preview. The next engineering phase starts with the shared document schema and local application foundation.

Where does AI fit into the workflow?

AI is planned as an optional collaborator. It proposes scoped edits with an explanation and preview. You decide what becomes part of your project; manual authoring and the deterministic core come first.

Who is it being designed for?

Independent visual storytellers and small teams making motion comics, animatics, and branching episodic stories. The initial focus is a coherent, controllable creative workflow.

What happens to my original work?

The planned architecture preserves source assets, explicit dependencies, revision history, and reversible commands. A generated image remains an image; editable layers and rigs are editable only when their source data supports it.
